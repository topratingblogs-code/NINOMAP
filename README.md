NINOMAP – El Niño Warning Levels Map
This interactive map displays El Niño warning levels based on the PAGASA classification of sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies in the Pacific. 
The map  is hosted publicly via https://topratingblogs-code.github.io/NINOMAP/. It
is intended as a simple visual reference for understanding the intensity of El Niño conditions across the mapped area.

The map uses four warning levels, each shown with a corresponding color: Weak (Green), Moderate (Yellow), Strong (Orange), and Very Strong (Red). 
These levels are defined by how much the sea surface temperature deviates from normal, following PAGASA's standard classification.

Weak (Gray-1) corresponds to an anomaly of 0.5°C to 0.9°C above normal. Conditions are near normal, drought risk is low, and water supply is generally adequate. Farmers may proceed with normal planting but should still adopt water-saving practices such as alternate wetting and drying (AWD) and monitor weather advisories. 
Everyone should stay informed and practice regular water conservation.

Moderate (Yellow-2) corresponds to an anomaly of 1.0°C to 1.4°C above normal. 
Drought risk is increasing and water supply may become strained. Farmers should coordinate with their local government unit for an adjusted planting calendar, secure crop insurance, and consider early-maturing, drought-tolerant varieties. 
Households should begin conserving water and monitor official advisories.

Strong (Orange-3) corresponds to an anomaly of 1.5°C to 1.9°C above normal. Serious drought effects are likely, water shortages are expected, and crops are at high risk. Farmers should temporarily shift to less water-intensive crops such as corn, mungbean, watermelon, or pechay. 
Local government units should prepare for water rationing, deploy water tankers if needed, and intensify health and fire-risk monitoring.

Very Strong (Red-4) corresponds to an anomaly of 2.0°C or more above normal, sometimes referred to as "Super El Niño." Drought and water shortage are expected to be severe, and conditions are critical. 
Farmers should postpone rice planting and explore alternative livelihood activities including livestock raising.
Households should prepare for significant water disruptions and follow emergency water distribution schedules. Local government units should implement full drought contingency plans and prioritize water for drinking and health services.

Note: The color scale above is an interpretation of the risk levels used in Philippine El Niño vulnerability maps.
